Developing Conceptual Understanding Through the Use of Computer Based
Mathematics

The main objective of this presentation will be to present the findings of a
research study conducted on community college mathematics students with the
integration of Prentice Hall's Interactive Math (1997).  The conceptual
framework underlying this study will be presented, which was a union of
andragogy and situated learning.  Andragogy is a set of principles and
strategies for teaching adult students (Cross, 1981; Knowles, 1990), and
situated learning is a learning model that suggests that meaningful learning
will occur only if it is embedded in the a `real world' context (Lave & Wenger,
1991).

Students were taught through computer assisted instruction coupled with a
reform environment and compared to students taught in a traditional lecture
environment.  Students taught in the computerized environment had better rates
of retention, reported increased self confidence toward mathematics and
decreased mathematics anxiety.  Additionally, the students in the computerized
environment surpassed the traditional students in conceptual understanding and
maintained the same level of procedural skill.
