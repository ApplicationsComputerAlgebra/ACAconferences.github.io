                         Cholesky in Chains 

                         Rick Palmer
                         rick@cs.cornell.edu

Abstract:

The Chains algebraic-topological programming language is being
developed to provide an elegant means for expressing efficient
scientific computations.  The basic datatypes in Chains are
implementations of mathematical objects from combinatorial and
algebraic topology, such as simplicial complexes and k-chains defined
over them.  In this talk I will describe how these topological
datatypes provide a concise, formal means of specifying and executing
a variety of scientific calculations, including automatic mesh
generation, finite element calculations, and linear systems solving.

In particular, I will describe how Chains has been used solve a finite
element approximation, including 1) a concise (one page) specification
of the finite element method using simplicial complexes, k-chains and
simplicial maps, 2) a Chains specification of a guaranteed quality
triangulation algorithm due to Paul Chew, and 3) a specification of
the Cholesky decomposition algorithm for sparse positive definite
linear systems, written in terms of (surprise!) simplicial complexes,
k-chains and simplicial maps.
