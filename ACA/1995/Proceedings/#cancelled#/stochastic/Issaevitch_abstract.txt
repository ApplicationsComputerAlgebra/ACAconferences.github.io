Computer Algebra in Stochastic System Identification.
-----------------------------------------------------
Tom Issaevitch, Wolfram Research

The problem of identifying general state-space linear systems using a
consistent statistical framework and current algorithms results in a
nonlinear optimization problem that is difficult to solve.  In
particular, for high-dimensional systems, the situation is hopeless.
As a result, a number of ad-hoc approaches have been developed which are
tractable, but have no rigorous statistical basis.  In particlar, the
Ho-Kalman realization algorithm requires only singular value decomposition.
However, the Kalman-Ho algorithm does not arise from any statistical
proceedure (e.g., maximum likelihood) and so there is no way to judge it's
optimality or accuracy.

In my talk, I will describe the use of Mathematica in performing a
perturbative identification of state space models using the maximum likelihood
method under the assumption of Guassian noise.  In addition to providing
full statistical information about the estimated parameters, the method 
uses singular value decomposition and so retains most of the computational
simplicity of the Kalman-Ho algorithm.  In particular, the method
does not suffer from the problem of spurious local minima common to
nonlinear methods in high dimensions.
