Computer Algebra in the Life Sciences

Moderator: Michael P. Barnett, Professor Emeritus Computer and Information Science, CUNY

At least 200 recent papers on clinical medicine, bioengineering, public health, population dynamics, population and evolutionary genetics, cell physiology, biochemical kinetics and stereochemistry and biostatistics report results that were obtained with the aid of computer algebra (symbolic computation) software such as Maple, and the underlying mathematics. These enable the computer to differentiate and integrate mathematical expressions and to solve equations symbolically. The professional practice of the life sciences, including teaching and research, will benefit from computer algebra to an ever increasing extent.

In this session, a group of panelists will

  1. Identify a selection of mathematical problems that occur in applications computer algebra that have been reported, and the practical contexts in which these occur;
  2. Discuss further mathematical problems that occur in several areas of the life sciences, including drug design, delivery and action;
  3. Discuss research and educational strategies to help fulfill the potential social benefits of computer algebra via its use in the life sciences.

Speakers will include computer scientists and mathematicians who work on life science problems and life scientists who use / would like to use computer algebra. Audience participation will be a key element of the session. Further details, including panel members, will be announced shortly.
