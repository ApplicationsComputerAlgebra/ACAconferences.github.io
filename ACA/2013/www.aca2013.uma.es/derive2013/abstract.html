Description of Derive's Special Session

DERIVETM 6 is a powerful system for doing symbolic and numeric mathematics. It processes algebraic variables, expressions, equations, functions, vectors, matrices and Boolean expressions in the same way as a scientific calculator processes numbers.

Problems in the fields of arithmetic, algebra, trigonometry, calculus, linear algebra, and propositional calculus can be solved with a click of the mouse. Plots of mathematical expressions in two and three dimensions using various coordinate systems can be easily performed. Furthermore, the use of the slide bar utility allows beautiful presentations of plot movement and makes it possible to study plots depending on different parameters.

The seamless integration of numeric, algebraic and graphic capabilities makes DERIVE 6 an excellent tool not only for learning or teaching but also for doing mathematics in many applications.

The new TI-NSPIRETM CAS technology makes it possible to recognize, simplify and calculate mathematical expressions, preserving the symbols including variables and terms.

This ability to enter and view equations and expressions on screen as they appear in textbooks may be especially useful for step-by-step, arithmetic, algebraic and calculus calculations.

Although DERIVE is discontinued, and TI-NSPIRE CAS has been proclaimed as its successor, there are many professionals who are still using DERIVE. For example, in TIME 2010 conference (www.time2010.uma.es), organized by us, there were more than 30 proposals directly related with DERIVE or TI-NSPIRE CAS.

The main purpose of this Special Session is to share the applications and libraries developed in DERIVE or TI-NSPIRE CAS. Papers about the use of DERIVE or TI-NSPIRE CAS in different disciplines, the development of specific libraries, experiences involving DERIVE or TI-NSPIRE CAS are welcome. Lectures using calculators (TI-92, Voyage 200, TI-NSPIRE CAS, CASIO, ...), smart phones or mobile devices running CAS are also welcome.

Selected papers presented in this session will be published in a special issue of the Journal of Symbolic Computation (JSC), an Elsevier journal with impact factor in JCR (Science Edition).
